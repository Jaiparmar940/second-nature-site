How it works

Start with the job. Then earn autonomy.

STEP 01

Walk one station.

Show us the task, the pace, the exceptions, and what a good shift looks like.

STEP 02

Run it with an operator.

A trained person controls the robot remotely from the first shift. The work gets done while we collect training data.

STEP 03

Step back over time.

Routine actions move toward autonomy. The operator stays available for judgment and new edge cases.

Human supervision

When the robot hesitates, a person takes over.

A jammed item, a bad seal, or something new should not stop the shift. The operator sees the station, guides the robot through the case, and returns control when the job is ready.

Each intervention becomes an example for the next similar case. The goal is fewer takeovers, not a floor with no people.

Frequently asked

The questions an operations team should ask.

Plain answers for evaluating a first robot-operated shift.

What exactly am I hiring?

A robot-operated shift for one defined job. We bring the robot, train the task, provide remote operation, and stay involved as the work moves toward greater autonomy.

What kinds of work are a good first fit?

Bounded, repetitive station work with clear inputs and outputs. Overnight quality checks, sorting, packing, and defined rework are the jobs we want to walk first.

What happens when the robot gets stuck?

A remote operator takes control, works through the case, verifies the result, and returns the robot to the routine task. The intervention is saved as training data.

Do I need my team to operate it?

No. Your team shows us the workflow and owns the requirements for the station. We remotely operate the robot throughout each shift, so you do not need to assign a dedicated robot operator.

Does every station qualify?

We begin with stations that match the robot's current reach, cycle time, safety limits, and ability to handle variation. As training improves, we are expanding the range of roles and station types we can support.

How does pricing work?

The pilot includes a one-time deployment and training fee, followed by a monthly rate per robot-shift. We target a rate below the loaded cost of the unfilled role. Additional training and task execution are available for an added fee. Alternatively, the robot can be purchased outright for a one-time charge, with a small recurring subscription for model access.

How do we get started?

Walk us through the missing shift: the station, normal cycle, edge cases, and staffing gap. If it fits, we will propose one bounded three-month pilot.
