Robots, trained for your job. Working from day one.

What we do

A capable robot still has to learn your job, at your site.

We bring a generalist robot to your facility and train it for one specific role. A trained remote operator runs it from the first shift, so the work gets done while the robot learns your items, your pace, and your edge cases.

Every operator action becomes training data. As the robot gets more reliable, the operator moves from doing each task to supervising the work. The goal is a robot that can run the job on its own without changing what you pay per shift.

Starting with food processing and packing facilities in California's Central Valley.
How it works

The job gets done now. The robot learns on site.

01 / FIRST SHIFT

Day one: it works.

We place a robot at your facility. A trained person operates it remotely. The job gets done from the first shift while we learn how the work really happens.

02 / EACH SHIFT

Every shift, it learns.

Each operator action teaches the robot about your tasks, your items, and the cases that do not go as planned. Training comes from the real job at your site.

03 / OVER TIME

Over time: it runs itself.

As performance improves, the operator steps back to supervising. You pay the same monthly rate. The robot gets better at the bounded job until it can handle routine work on its own.

Readiness has to be measured.

Our first open benchmark uses 5 vehicle electrical scenarios with 5 runs each and scores the outcome. It is not a robot deployment; it shows how we build repeatable tests and document limits. Showing 7 of 9 models; full table in the repo.

Each five-run cell carries wide uncertainty. The tier-level pattern is more reliable than any single score. Full methodology is in the repo.
